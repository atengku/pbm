Read this first — what this record shows

Since Bitcoin was introduced to the world, central banks have — quietly, in papers the public was never shown — been building digital ledgers of their own. In Singapore that work began with Project Ubin in 2016, and twenty-one official papers have followed since the first, every one of them hosted on this site. This library exists to put that record in front of the public: purpose-bound money and CBDC are being positioned as the future of currency — and Singapore, under the pretext of assistance, has been running the live test on its own population through the voucher schemes.

Understand what the end goal of this architecture is. A conventional central bank controls the supply of money. A central bank issuing programmable money controls its velocity — money with a spend-by date is money that must move when the issuer decides, at the shops the issuer permits, or be extinguished. That capability is not speculation; it is specified in MAS's own whitepaper below, expiry and revocation included.

And when you are told Singapore does not print money from thin air — read the record on this page, watch the grey money-supply curve rising behind the timeline, and form your own opinion.

Original White Paper

Purpose Bound Money (PBM) — Technical Whitepaper

June 2023

MAS, with IMF, Amazon, DBS, JPMorgan Onyx, Bank of Korea & others

The founding technical specification of Purpose Bound Money. Defines the two-part model — a smart-contract wrapper that encodes usage conditions (validity/expiry period, merchant whitelists, denominations) around an underlying store-of-value token (CBDC, tokenised deposits, or regulated stablecoins). Establishes PBM as a bearer instrument with self-contained logic, transferable without intermediaries, and later formalised as Ethereum standard ERC-7291. This is the reference document for how programmable conditions — including the expiry behaviour built into government vouchers — are specified and enforced.
