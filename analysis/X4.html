01What this is

“Central bank digital currencies: foundational principles and core features,” a report by seven central banks (Bank of Canada, Bank of England, Bank of Japan, ECB, Federal Reserve, Sveriges Riksbank, Swiss National Bank) and the BIS, published 9 Oct 2020. Co-chairs: Sir Jon Cunliffe (BoE) and Benoît Cœuré (BIS Innovation Hub); the governors' group is chaired by Christine Lagarde, now ECB President. Three principles — coexistence with cash, do no harm, innovation/efficiency — plus core features and an explicit “no opinion on whether to issue.”

02Why it matters

This is the moment CBDC moves from national experiments to coordinated bloc doctrine — the last timeline stop before Singapore's build goes fully public. And the cast recirculates: Cœuré from X2, Lagarde from X3, now signing with larger mandates. The MAS optionality posture P3 here becomes shared G7-bloc doctrine.

03Key findings

Finding 1 — The cast recirculates. Cœuré arrives from d174 X2; Lagarde from the Singapore speech X3, now a central-bank governor. The official arc is a small, continuous cast acquiring larger mandates — the pattern the MAS corpus shows at consortium level P10.
Finding 2 — Optionality goes global. “No opinion on whether to issue” plus a joint capability roadmap is precisely the have-it-ready-decide-later posture MAS codifies thirteen months later P3. What Singapore practised nationally, the bloc adopts as shared doctrine.
Finding 3 — The private-sector clause is the banks' insurance. “An appropriate role for the private sector” enshrines two-tier distribution — the structural guarantee that any CBDC arrives through, not around, incumbent banks. It is the same protective choice behind PBM's tokenised-bank-liability collateral G5.

05The Money Trail

No commercial transaction, but the doctrine is the banks' insurance policy: the “appropriate role for the private sector” clause guarantees CBDC routes through incumbent banks, protecting the deposit franchise — the same logic that later makes tokenised bank liabilities win the money race G5. The influence trail is personnel: Lagarde (IMF → ECB) and Cœuré (CPMI → BIS Innovation Hub) carry the arc from X2 / X3 into bloc doctrine, nowhere flagged as continuity. Meanwhile the population-scale test was already running — Singapore's CDC vouchers launched in 2020, ahead of this report. Singapore was not implementing the doctrine; the doctrine was catching up to Singapore.

06The Gap

Measured against its own principles P1, the deployed voucher stack makes an uncomfortable scorecard. (1) “Coexistence with cash”: vouchers are app-only, paper letters discontinued. (2) “Do no harm”: ~44% breakage reported on a cost-of-living scheme. (3) “Clear legal framework”: no published accounting standard for expired value, no dispute state in the citizen lifecycle. The principles are the establishment's own yardstick; the brief's findings are what the yardstick measures.
