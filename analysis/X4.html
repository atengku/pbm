Stage 1 — Intake Analysis

SUBJECT: "Central bank digital currencies: foundational principles and core features" — report by seven central banks (Bank of Canada, Bank of England, Bank of Japan, ECB, Federal Reserve, Sveriges Riksbank, Swiss National Bank) and the BIS, published 9 Oct 2020. Co-chairs: Sir Jon Cunliffe (BoE) and Benoît Cœuré (BIS Innovation Hub). Governors' group chaired by Christine Lagarde, now ECB President. SOURCE / RELIABILITY: Primary — BIS publication othp33 + press release. Highest tier. KEY CLAIMS: three foundational principles — coexistence with cash, do no harm to monetary and financial stability, promotion of innovation and efficiency. Core features: resilient and secure; convenient at very low or no cost; underpinned by a clear legal framework; an appropriate role for the private sector. Explicitly "does not give an opinion on whether to issue"; the group commits to continued joint work "without prejudging any decision." SIGNIFICANCE: the moment CBDC moves from national experiments to coordinated bloc doctrine — the last stop on the timeline before Singapore's build goes fully public. ROUTE: → timeline anchor, Oct 2020.

Stage 2 — Knowledge Map

Executive Summary (Alphas)

Alpha 1 — The cast recirculates. Cœuré arrives from d174 [X2]; Lagarde arrives from the Singapore speech [X3], now signing as a central-bank governor. The official arc is a small, continuous cast acquiring larger mandates — the same pattern the MAS corpus shows at the consortium level [P5][P10]. Alpha 2 — Optionality goes global. "No opinion on whether to issue" plus a joint capability roadmap is precisely the have-it-ready-decide-later posture MAS codifies thirteen months later in its retail-CBDC monograph [P3]. What Singapore practised as a national strategy, the G7 bloc here adopts as shared doctrine. Alpha 3 — The private-sector clause is the banks' insurance. "An appropriate role for the private sector" enshrines two-tier distribution — the structural guarantee that any CBDC arrives through, not around, the incumbent banks. It is the same protective choice that later determines PBM's collateral design (tokenised bank liabilities win the money race [G5]).

Stage 3 — Research Note

The hinge — and what was already running

Oct 2020 is the timeline's hinge: after it, the question is engineering, not legitimacy. But note what the brief's chronology puts immediately before it: Singapore's CDC voucher scheme launched in 2020, months ahead of the principles report. The population-scale test of conditional digital disbursement was already running while the G7 was still writing first principles. Singapore was not implementing the doctrine; the doctrine was catching up to Singapore.

Read against the principles themselves, the deployed voucher stack makes an uncomfortable scorecard: "coexistence with cash" — vouchers are app-only, letters discontinued; "do no harm" — 44% breakage on a cost-of-living scheme; "clear legal framework" — no published accounting standard for expired value, no dispute state in the citizen lifecycle P1. The principles are the establishment's own yardstick; the brief's findings are what the yardstick measures.
