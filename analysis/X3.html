Stage 1 — Intake Analysis

SUBJECT: "Winds of Change: The Case for New Digital Currency" — speech by Christine Lagarde, IMF Managing Director, delivered at the Singapore Fintech Festival, 14 Nov 2018, with companion IMF Staff Discussion Note "Casting Light on Central Bank Digital Currency" (SDN 18/08). SOURCE / RELIABILITY: Primary — imf.org transcript and published SDN. Highest tier. KEY CLAIMS: demand for cash is dwindling; the state has a continuing role in supplying money to the digital economy; "I believe we should consider the possibility to issue digital currency"; framed on financial inclusion, security and consumer privacy; proposes public-private partnership — the central bank provides settlement, the private sector runs the interface and innovation. SIGNIFICANCE: eight months after the BIS's cautious d174 [X2], the IMF's chief moves the question from whether to how — and does it on MAS's home stage. ROUTE: → timeline anchor, Nov 2018.

Stage 2 — Knowledge Map

Executive Summary (Alphas)

Alpha 1 — The permission speech. D174 said "consider carefully" [X2]; Lagarde says "we should consider issuing." The Overton window of official money moves in eight months, and the direction never reverses afterwards. Alpha 2 — The venue is the tell. Of all stages, the case for state digital currency is made at the Singapore Fintech Festival — MAS's flagship event, two years into Ubin [P5]–[P7]. The location anoints Singapore as the laboratory jurisdiction in front of the global fintech industry. Five years later, MAS ships the PBM whitepaper [P1]; the IMF is a named co-developer of its architecture. Alpha 3 — The privacy pitch, and where it landed. The speech sells state digital currency partly as privacy protection against data-hungry private platforms. Trace the corpus forward: the world-class privacy engineering that actually got built protects bank data in wholesale rails (Mandala's zero-knowledge proofs [P13]); the retail citizen got a lifecycle that ends at Expired with no dispute state [P1]. The promise was made to the population; the engineering was delivered to the institutions.

Stage 3 — Research Note

One person, one arc

Follow the speaker: Nov 2018, Lagarde asks the question in Singapore. Oct 2020, as President of the ECB, she chairs the group of governors that signs the answer — the seven-central-bank foundational principles report [X4]. Jun 2023, MAS ships the wrapper specification with the IMF at the table [P1]. One official spans the entire officialisation arc, and Singapore threads every stage of it: the stage where the case was made, the pilot jurisdiction while the principles were drafted, and the first mover to a deployed national scheme.

For the brief's argument the speech matters as the moment conditional digital money acquired its humanitarian vocabulary — inclusion, safety, privacy. Every subsequent defence of the voucher stack inherits that vocabulary. The gap between the vocabulary and the deployed design — expiry undisclosed, no appeal path, breakage unaccounted P1 — is the exposure the brief documents.
