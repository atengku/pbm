01What this is

“Winds of Change: The Case for New Digital Currency,” a speech by Christine Lagarde, IMF Managing Director, delivered at the Singapore Fintech Festival on 14 Nov 2018, with the companion IMF Staff Discussion Note “Casting Light on Central Bank Digital Currency” (SDN 18/08). Its core line: “I believe we should consider the possibility to issue digital currency” — framed on inclusion, security and privacy, proposing a public-private split where the central bank settles and the private sector runs the interface.

02Why it matters

Eight months after the BIS's cautious d174 X2, the IMF's chief moves the question from whether to how — and does it on MAS's flagship stage, two years into Ubin P5. The venue anoints Singapore as the laboratory jurisdiction in front of the global fintech industry. Five years later MAS ships PBM P1 with the IMF a named co-developer of its architecture.

03Key findings

Finding 1 — The permission speech. D174 said “consider carefully” X2; Lagarde says “we should consider issuing.” The Overton window of official money moves in eight months, and the direction never reverses.
Finding 2 — The venue is the tell. Of all stages, the case for state digital currency is made at the Singapore Fintech Festival — MAS's own event — anointing the pilot jurisdiction publicly, in front of the industry.
Finding 3 — The privacy pitch, and where it landed. Sold partly as privacy protection against data-hungry platforms; the privacy engineering that actually shipped protects bank data in wholesale rails (Mandala's zero-knowledge proofs P13), while the retail citizen got a lifecycle ending at Expired with no dispute state P1. The promise was made to the population; the engineering was delivered to institutions.

05The Money Trail

No transaction, but a clear positioning trail: the IMF makes the case on MAS's stage, then becomes a named co-developer of the very scheme MAS eventually ships P1. The individual is the through-line — Lagarde delivers the question at the IMF, then signs the answer at the ECB X4. The non-disclosure sits in the distance between the humanitarian vocabulary sold to the population (inclusion, safety, privacy) and the deployed design that delivered privacy to institutions P13 and expiry to citizens P1. Every subsequent defence of the voucher stack inherits the vocabulary minted here.

06The Gap

Three silences. (1) Privacy for whom: the privacy promise made to citizens vs the wholesale-only privacy engineering actually built P13 vs P1. (2) The interface handoff: the public-private split leaves the interface — where citizen experience actually lives — to private operators, unexamined. (3) The unfollowed arc: the path from “consider issuing” to a deployed conditional-money scheme is never traced by the speaker, though she signs the next stage herself X4.
