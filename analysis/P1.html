01What this is

The founding technical specification of Purpose Bound Money. It defines a two-part design: a smart-contract wrapper that encodes conditions — validity/expiry window, merchant whitelist, denominations — around an underlying store-of-value token (a CBDC, a tokenised bank deposit, or a regulated stablecoin) used as collateral. The money underneath stays "dumb"; all the rules live in the wrapper. Later standardised as Ethereum ERC-7291.

02Why it matters

This is the reference document for every "can the system bend?" question about Singapore's voucher schemes. Sections 3.4–3.5 (the expiry and revoke flows) are the technical spine of the entire policy-vs-architecture argument: they prove the conditions citizens experience are authored, enforced by code, and reversible by the creator — never inevitable.

03Key findings

Finding 1 — The wrapper IS the policy surface. Every constraint a citizen meets (expiry, merchant lists, denominations) lives in the wrapper, authored by the PBM Creator. The money layer is deliberately kept unprogrammed. Whoever writes the wrapper writes the policy — without the scrutiny a statutory instrument would get.
Finding 2 — Revocation exists in the reference design. Figure 15: the creator can revoke expired PBMs and recover the underlying collateral. "The system cannot override an expiry" is therefore an operating-policy stance, never a protocol impossibility. The claw-back path is in MAS's own spec.
Finding 3 — Privacy-by-structure is a pre-built defence. Creator ≠ money-issuer separation is MAS's engineered answer to the "surveillance money" attack line — built before the attack arrived. It tells you public sentiment was war-gamed years ahead.

05The Money Trail

The paper presents eleven institutions as neutral "collaborators." Read as a money map, most are positioned to monetise the very system the paper standardises: the banks (DBS, OCBC, Onyx/JPM) settle the tokenised deposits PBM rides on; the vendors (Amazon, Grab, Fazz, NETS) supply the cloud, wallets and acceptance rails; OGP operates the live disbursement machinery. Of the eleven, the commercial interest is disclosed for only the regulators and central banks. The single ■ is Onyx by J.P. Morgan — a private bank's blockchain unit co-authoring a public-money standard that its own commercial network (the Partior lineage, per P10) then sells into, with no disclosure in-paper of that downstream position. The trail from "co-author of the standard" to "vendor of the rails" is exactly the line this register is built to make visible; it points onward to the consortium map in P10 and P12.

06The Gap

Three things the paper never says. (1) Breakage economics: expired value reverts to the creator — in a government scheme, the state — yet no MAS document names who books that value or under what accounting standard. PBM makes breakage programmable and precise, and the paper is silent on it. (2) No dispute state: the lifecycle ends at "Expired" with no appeal path in the protocol; the citizen's only remedy is off-chain administrative discretion. (3) Wrapper authorship as delegated legislation: conditions in a government PBM are rules with real force, authored as code by agencies and vendors, without the scrutiny statutory instruments receive.
