01What this is

Guardian's foundational framework, co-authored by MAS (Alan Lim, lead) and the BIS (Tara Rice, Shirakami, Hancock). It defines digital-asset network archetypes, applies FMI principles to token networks, and casts regulated FIs as trust anchors issuing verifiable credentials for participation. Three interoperability approaches are named (common infrastructure / layered global base / interlinked networks). Contributors include DBS, HSBC, StanChart, UOB, SBI DAH, Marketnode and JPMorgan (Naveen Mallela, Toh Wee Kee).

02Why it matters

This is the doctrine GL1 P12 and the rest of Guardian inherit — and its authorship is the corpus's clearest revolving-door specimen. Trust-anchor credentialing extends the bank franchise from money to identity itself: participation in tokenised markets becomes downstream of a bank relationship. The Blueprint gated who programs money P4; G7 gates who exists on-network.

03Key findings

Finding 1 — Trust anchors = the licensing of identity. Making regulated FIs the issuers of verifiable credentials gates who exists on-network, not just who programs money. Trust-anchor failure or de-platforming has no appeal path — the recourse gap recurs at the identity layer.
Finding 2 — The revolving door has a name. Toh Wee Kee authored Ubin Phase 1 at MAS (2017) P7, then appears here as a JPMorgan contributor (2023). With Naveen Mallela's decade-long JPM constancy, the public-private membrane in Singapore digital money is visibly permeable — the signer of public research now signs the bank's framework built on it.
Finding 3 — The Metcalfe concession, overridden. The paper candidly admits public networks reach millions while private networks reach thousands — then the programme builds permissioned “public permissioned” anyway. The trade-off is acknowledged and overridden without a stated price.

05The Money Trail

The paper reads as neutral doctrine, but its authors and contributors are precisely the actors positioned to become the trust anchors the doctrine anoints — the banks that co-write the credential model become its licensed issuers, extending the bank franchise to identity. The sharpest non-disclosure is biographical: Toh Wee Kee co-authored MAS's Ubin foundation P7, then signs a JPMorgan-contributed framework built on the same doctrine — the revolving door instantiated, affiliation stated but the conflict never flagged. Read alongside Mallela's JPM constancy, the public-private membrane appears (Tier-3: appears, on the named record) permeable by design. Onward to the consortium map P12.

06The Gap

Three silences. (1) Trust-anchor liability: the framework makes FIs the gatekeepers of on-network existence but leaves their liability for wrongful de-platforming unwritten. (2) No appeal path: credential failure or removal has no recourse route — the identity-layer version of the citizen's missing dispute state P1. (3) The Metcalfe price: the cost of choosing permissioned over public reach is admitted, then never quantified.
