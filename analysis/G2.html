01What this is

A non-prescriptive framework for tokenised funds under Project Guardian. It maps the fund lifecycle (issuance → distribution → secondary → custody → servicing), sets out three register archetypes (mirror / partial on-chain / full on-chain authoritative), and introduces the Guardian Composable Token Taxonomy (GCTT), with a tokenised money-market-fund case study. It openly admits that tokenising existing fund structures yields capped benefits and that the real prize needs fund structures that do not yet exist in law.

02Why it matters

The register question — when does the chain become the authoritative record — is the whole legal ballgame, because authority over the record is authority over the fees, the liability and the veto. G2 names the ladder; its sibling paper climbs it. The framework standardises a transitional product while the endgame stays unlegislated, and it does so without ever naming the party it displaces (the transfer-agency and fund-administration franchise) or pricing the retail-privacy cost of ledgered personalisation.

03Key findings

Finding 1 — Authority migration is the real product. Mirror → partial → full on-chain registers is a ladder of legal authority moving from administrator to ledger. Efficiency and personalisation are decoration on the only question that matters: who holds the authoritative record, and therefore the fees, the liability and the veto.
Finding 2 — The capped-benefit admission. The framework concedes that tokenising today's fund structures delivers limited gains; the promised land requires new structures. Translation: it standardises a transitional product while the endgame remains unlegislated.
Finding 3 — GCTT is a taxonomy land-grab. As with the ICMA taxonomy in G1, whoever's composable token taxonomy becomes default owns the integration layer of a trillion-dollar industry's plumbing.

05The Money Trail

The prize here is the authoritative register. Every rung of the mirror → partial → full ladder moves legal authority — and with it the fees, the liability and the veto — from the fund administrator to whoever operates the ledger. The framework never names the incumbent it disrupts (the transfer-agency and fund-admin franchise), so the displacement reads as neutral modernisation rather than a transfer of economic rent. Whoever wins GCTT adoption collects the integration tax across the industry. And there is an unpriced citizen cost: direct-to-investor personalisation on an immutable ledger builds a permanent behavioural record of retail investors, with no privacy treatment anywhere in the paper. The authority-migration ladder carries straight into the legal deep-dive of G6, sits beside the fixed-income taxonomy play in G1, and the industry's own US$16tn sizing lives in G7.

06The Gap

What the framework leaves unwritten. (1) The displaced party: the transfer-agency and fund-admin incumbents whose franchise the register migration erodes are never named. (2) Register bifurcation: mirror and partial models create two records that can disagree — a reconciliation failure in a "tokenised" fund would be a scandal precisely because the tech promised its impossibility, yet the risk is unpriced. (3) The personalisation-privacy trade: ledgered direct-to-investor personalisation builds a permanent behavioural record of retail investors, with no privacy treatment at all.
