01What this is

A New York Fed (NYIC) × MAS experiment settling wholesale cross-border FX through vehicle currencies across heterogeneous DLT ledgers via HTLC — atomic settlement in under 30 seconds across multi-hop payment chains. The report carries the heaviest disclaimers in the corpus: no policy signal, no CBDC decision implied.

02Why it matters

As research it extends Jasper–Ubin P8 incrementally; as statecraft it is declarative. Pairing MAS with the New York Fed anchors Singapore as the Fed’s default Asian counterpart on dollar-bridged wholesale settlement, and the illiquid-EME-pair framing targets exactly the corridors that correspondent de-risking P9 abandoned. The science re-treads 2019; the positioning is new.

03Key findings

Finding 1 — Vehicle currency = the US dollar, formalised. Routing every illiquid pair through a bridge currency enshrines the dollar (and, by courtesy, SGD) in the settlement layer itself — dollarisation as protocol design, wearing neutrality language.
Finding 2 — The partnership is the payload. Three years after Jasper–Ubin proved HTLC atomicity, re-proving it with the New York Fed adds little science and much positioning: MAS becomes the Fed’s standing counterpart in wholesale digital-dollar research.
Finding 3 — Sub-30-seconds is the first real performance number. The corpus’s chronic omission — latency and cost versus a correspondent baseline — finally gets a datum: under 30 seconds across multi-hop chains, honestly labelled as running on toy infrastructure (small VMs). Candid about scale.

05The Money Trail

The report is framed as a neutral central-bank experiment, and its authors — the NYIC and MAS — are genuinely public. The commercial payload is structural rather than named: routing every illiquid pair through a vehicle currency hard-codes the US dollar into the settlement layer, a positioning that benefits dollar-clearing incumbents while wearing the language of neutrality. The experiment also cannot ask who market-makes the bridge leg, or at what spread — the liquidity-provision economics of on-chain vehicle currencies is the commercial question it is structurally barred from posing. That the heaviest disclaimers in the whole corpus sit on this report is itself the signal of how sensitive Fed-adjacent CBDC work is. It re-uses the HTLC method of P8 and aims at the de-risked corridors of P9.

06The Gap

Three gaps. (1) Protocol-level dollar entrenchment: no participating author can name the dollarisation the design bakes in, so none does. (2) Liquidity economics: who provides and prices the vehicle-leg liquidity is unowned and unquantified. (3) Privacy and scale: privacy is explicitly unsolved (a public Sepolia testnet), and performance is proven only on toy VMs — the sub-30-second figure is a floor, not a production number.
