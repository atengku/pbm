01What this is

The founding design paper for Global Layer One (GL1): a shared "public permissioned" (Model 3) ledger for tokenised money and assets, operated by regulated financial institutions through GL1 Operating Companies. It sets out a three-model taxonomy — public-permissionless (rejected as unaccountable), private-permissioned (rejected as fragmenting), and public-permissioned (adopted) — hosting composable DvP/PvP/DvPvP settlement across jurisdictions, asset-agnostic, with FI-operated nodes and KYC'd participants. Contributing institutions named alongside MAS are BNY, Citi, MUFG, JPMorgan's Onyx and Société Générale-FORGE, with BIS "Finternet"/Unified Ledger framing cited as intellectual cover.

02Why it matters

GL1 is where the domestic concession pattern becomes a global one. Project Dunbar concluded that no sovereign will host its liability on a shared platform it does not control (P14); the Orchid Blueprint answered the domestic version by designating who operates the ledger and who may deploy wrappers (P4). GL1 answers the international version by incorporating a company — turning a governance vacuum into a cap table. Whoever holds the Operating-Company seats owns the settlement layer of tokenised finance. The paper is written in the language of open networks; the power structure it describes is a franchise.

03Key findings

Finding 1 — "Public permissioned" is a franchise worded as openness. The network is "open to any entity that fulfils the criteria" — but the criteria are set by the incumbents operating the nodes. Model 3 rhetoric imports internet-scale legitimacy while the KYC gate keeps internet-scale participation out. The openness describes the membership form, not the power structure.
Finding 2 — The Operating Company is Dunbar's missing operator, incorporated. Dunbar proved sovereigns will not cede control to a shared platform. GL1's answer is an industry utility run by a bank consortium — the governance vacuum becomes a company, and the five contributors appear to be the presumptive shareholders of tokenised finance's common rail.
Finding 3 — The antitrust surface is untouched. A bank-owned utility that sets participation criteria for its own competitors is a competition-law question. It appears nowhere in the paper. The five banks are already "at the table" designing the rules of a market they will also trade in; that positioning is undisclosed as a conflict.

05The Money Trail

Read as a money map, GL1 is a seat allocation. The five contributing banks — BNY, Citi, MUFG, Onyx/JPMorgan and SocGen-FORGE — are positioned to become the shareholders of the utility that settles tokenised money and assets across borders; the fee and float pool of that settlement layer is the prize, and the industry sizes the addressable tokenisation market in the trillions (the Guardian track cites the US$16tn-by-2030 forecast, see G7). The single undisclosed line each bank carries is that it co-authors the rulebook of a market it will also compete in. Onyx sits on both candidate fabrics — GL1 and the Partior lineage — with the lane-division unstated. The trail runs domestic-to-global: the Blueprint's designated-operator logic (P4) scaled up, Dunbar's operator vacuum (P14) filled by incorporation, and the Guardian frameworks (G1, G3) supplying the assets that will ride these rails.

06The Gap

Three things the paper never says. (1) The cap table: the Operating Company's incorporation status, domicile and shareholding are unspecified — the single fact that decides who owns tokenised finance's settlement layer is left blank. (2) The antitrust analysis: a bank-owned utility setting participation criteria for competitors is nowhere examined. (3) The sovereignty round-trip: Dunbar said sovereigns will not cede control to a shared platform; GL1 hands that control to private banks instead — solving the central-bank objection by creating a larger one for everyone outside the consortium.
