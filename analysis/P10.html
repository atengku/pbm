01What this is

The capstone of Project Ubin: "Ubin V," a multi-currency payments network built to production fidelity, authored by MAS with Temasek and J.P. Morgan and Accenture. It ships a public API set on github.com/project-ubin — including an ESCROW message set whose actions include release, revert and an explicit dispute — and frames the governance of common platforms ("trust is not binary") as the binding constraint.

02Why it matters

The shift from a consortium of eleven banks to a state-capital-plus-single-bank axis is the industrial-policy pivot of the entire programme. The stated purpose was proving value; the structural effect was selecting commercial champions. Within a year the Partior joint venture — JPMorgan, DBS and Temasek — launched with the multi-currency settlement mandate Ubin V prototyped, then returned as the contractor building BIS Dunbar prototypes P14.

03Key findings

Finding 1 — The exit was the point. Phase 1’s eleven-bank consortium becomes Phase 5’s MAS + Temasek + JPMorgan trio; within a year the same trio’s commercial echo, Partior (JPM/DBS/Temasek), launches with the mandate Ubin V prototyped. Public R&D to private JV, cleanly executed. Who captured the option value of five years of convened research is a fair and unasked question.
Finding 2 — Governance named as the real problem. The most durable line in the report: no single party is trusted to hold central-bank liabilities across borders, but "trust is not binary." That sentence is the intellectual seed of both Dunbar’s platform governance and GL1’s operating-company model.
Finding 3 — Escrow got a dispute verb; retail money did not. The public API ships ESCROW with release, revert and a dispute action — wholesale rails carry recourse in their message schema, while retail PBM P1 still ends at "Expired" with no dispute state. Recourse follows power.

05The Money Trail

Phase 5 presents itself as public research proving "value," but its structural effect was to select commercial champions. The eleven-bank consortium of Ubin Phase 1 narrows to MAS, Temasek and JPMorgan; within a year the Partior joint venture — JPMorgan, DBS and Temasek — launches with exactly the multi-currency settlement mandate Ubin V prototyped, and later returns as the contractor building BIS Dunbar prototypes P14. Public research → private company → public research’s vendor, inside twenty-four months. Temasek sits on both sides as state investor and JV shareholder; JPMorgan co-authors the public work and co-owns the commercial child; the paper names none of this downstream capture. This is the cleanest example in the corpus of convened public research resolving into a privately held product — the revolving door made infrastructural, pointing onward to the consortium map in P14 and P12.

06The Gap

Three unstated things. (1) No unit economics: the commercial answer arrived as a joint venture, not a table — no cost or pricing figure is published. (2) The Partior cap table: and MAS’s regulatory posture toward its own research’s commercial child, are undisclosed. (3) The dispute verb that never crossed over: the escrow schema carries a dispute action for banks, yet the retail PBM lifecycle P1 still has none — an asymmetry sitting in plain sight in MAS’s own specifications.
