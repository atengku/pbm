01What this is

A BIS Innovation Hub (Singapore Centre) project with the RBA, Bank Negara Malaysia, MAS and the South African Reserve Bank: a single shared platform hosting multiple CBDCs (the BIS "model 3") so central and commercial banks transact directly across borders, with default payment-versus-payment. Prototypes were built by R3 and Partior. The report ends, in its own words, "with more questions than answers."

02Why it matters

Dunbar is the maximal architecture and the honest record of why sovereigns resist it: no central bank will host its liability on infrastructure it does not control, and no operator exists that all trust. Its unsolved governance question is GL1’s founding inheritance P12, and Mandala P13 later tests its compliance proofs against Dunbar’s prototypes. The candour is the contribution.

03Key findings

Finding 1 — Sovereignty is the binding constraint, stated plainly. No central bank will let its liability be issued or recorded on infrastructure outside its control, and there is no natural operator all parties trust. Dunbar proves the technology and documents why the technology was never the problem — the same governance wall P8 and P10 hit, now with four flags planted on it.
Finding 2 — Partior building BIS prototypes closes the loop. The commercial JV born of Ubin V P10 returns as contractor to the multilateral successor programme. Public research → private company → public research’s vendor, in twenty-four months. The revolving door is infrastructural, not incidental.
Finding 3 — "More questions than before it started" is the finding. Dunbar’s candour converts it from a failed platform pitch into the field’s honest problem census — the direct predecessor of both Mandala (the compliance subset) and GL1 (the governance / operating-company subset).

05The Money Trail

Dunbar’s authors are public — the BIS Hub and four central banks — but its prototype builders are the tell. Partior, the JPMorgan/DBS/Temasek joint venture born out of Ubin Phase 5 P10, returns here as contractor to the multilateral successor programme: public research → private company → public research’s vendor, inside twenty-four months. The revolving door is not a personnel detail but infrastructural — the same commercial entity that captured the option value of Singapore’s convened research is paid to build the next multilateral prototype. The paper names Partior as a builder but discloses nothing of that lineage or downstream position. And the operator vacuum it documents so candidly then becomes the market opportunity GL1 P12 is designed to fill — Dunbar creates the demand for the very entity GL1 proposes.

06The Gap

Three unwritten things. (1) Exit mechanics: how a central bank leaves a shared platform is written nowhere — the analogue, at sovereign scale, of the missing unwind analysis in Ubin Phase 1 P5. (2) The operator vacuum: if no sovereign can operate it, a neutral operating company must; Dunbar creates the demand for that entity but names no cap table. (3) mBridge divergence: the geopolitical fragmentation of mCBDC platforms along political lines goes unmodelled.
