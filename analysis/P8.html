01What this is

The first cross-border, cross-currency, cross-platform atomic wholesale-CBDC payment: CAD on Corda (Jasper) settling against SGD on Quorum (Ubin) via Hash Time-Locked Contracts, with no trusted third party. All-or-nothing by construction. Delivered by the Bank of Canada and MAS with J.P. Morgan and Accenture as build partners.

02Why it matters

This paper establishes the bilateral-corridor model and, in the same breath, its ceiling. HTLC solves the two-party atom, not the N-jurisdiction network — the exact gap Project Dunbar P14 later attacks from the platform side and Project Mandala P13 from the compliance side. Its own open-questions list defines the agenda the next five years of BIS-track work worked through without fully closing.

03Key findings

Finding 1 — Trust was relocated, not removed. The paper admits it: HTLC shifts trust from correspondent banks into code plus timeout parameters. Timeout selection, platform bugs and protocol governance become the new counterparty risk — and it is left unpriced.
Finding 2 — The bilateral ceiling. HTLC scales as corridors, an N² problem, not as a network. Everything after this — Dunbar’s common platform, Mandala’s compliance proofs — is the programme discovering that limit and routing around it.
Finding 3 — The candour benchmark, and the constant private node. The open-questions list (governance, legality, scale) is the most honest in the Ubin corpus; later reports narrow these questions rather than answer them. J.P. Morgan, which built the Quorum leg, recurs across Ubin Phase 5 and the Onyx/Partior lineage — the constant private beneficiary of public corridor research.

05The Money Trail

The paper presents the Bank of Canada and MAS as neutral central-bank co-authors, with J.P. Morgan and Accenture as delivery partners. Read as a money map, the private positioning is unmistakable: J.P. Morgan built the Quorum ledger under the SGD leg, recurs across Ubin Phase 5 P10, and via the Onyx/Partior lineage commercialises the very multi-currency settlement design these corridors prototyped P14. Accenture bills integration across the phases. The corridor research is public; the vendor positions are private; and the paper discloses no downstream commercial interest for either. It appears the trust the design "removed" from correspondent banks was quietly re-lent to the firms that build and monetise the code.

06The Gap

Three things the paper never quantifies. (1) Timeout-parameter risk: a mis-set HTLC window recreates Herstatt settlement risk in miniature, and the analysis appears nowhere in the corpus. (2) Who runs the bridge: the interoperability layer between two sovereign ledgers has no proposed owner — the void GL1 P12 later claims. (3) No baseline: not one cost or latency figure versus the correspondent-banking benchmark, an omission that recurs until Cedar × Ubin’s sub-30-second claim P11.
