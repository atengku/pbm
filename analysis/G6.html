01What this is

The operational playbook for taking tokenised funds from pilot to production. It maps four legal structures (Digital Mirror / Twin / Native), analyses cross-chain settlement finality and bridged-token risk passage (Lock-&-Mint vs Burn-&-Mint) to insolvency grade, and carries five named use cases: Franklin Templeton, Phillip Securities (with stablecoin settlement), Fidelity+Citi (digital FX), Swift (TradFi bridge) and Deutsche Bank (DAMA 2). An enabler stack — KYC, network effects, ops controls, standards, UX — closes the paper.

02Why it matters

This is the corpus's most production-real document: global asset managers, Swift in the loop, insolvency-grade finality analysis. Legal structure precedes technical benefit, and the enabler stack bakes identity and KYC gating into the rails — compliance-by-design at the fund layer, with credentialed participation the precondition of access. Tokenisation stops being a prototype here.

03Key findings

Finding 1 — Finality is the whole ballgame. The moment a cross-chain transfer becomes irrevocable — for risk, tax, insolvency — is named as the critical unknown. G6 delivers the corpus's first insolvency-grade analysis of bridged tokens. The lawyers have arrived; that is the maturity signal.
Finding 2 — Swift inside the tokenised tent. The incumbent messaging monopoly appears as the TradFi-bridge use case — the same accommodation as Ubin's SWIFT diplomacy P10 and Mandala's ISO 20022 pathway P13. Every revolution in this corpus ends with the incumbent holding a workstream.
Finding 3 — Compliance built into the rails. The enabler stack makes KYC and credentialed participation a precondition of access. The stablecoin-settlement leg (Phillip case) quietly normalises private stablecoins in regulated fund settlement ahead of licensing volume — practice outrunning the whitelist P4.

05The Money Trail

Named managers convert Guardian from framework factory to distribution channel: MAS convening now moves real AUM logos — the credibility the adoption-gap paper G4 said was missing. Every named entity is positioned to monetise the production rails it helps define — managers tokenise their funds, Citi and Swift supply settlement and messaging, Deutsche Bank runs DAMA 2. The non-disclosure sits in two places: the stablecoin-settlement leg normalised ahead of SCS licensing P4, and the bridge-operator insolvency — who administers locked collateral when the bridge entity fails — left thinner than the token-level analysis. Compliance is engineered into the rails for the institutions; the citizen-facing recourse question is left downstream. Onward: G2, G3, P13.

06The Gap

Three things the playbook underspecifies. (1) Bridge-operator failure: token models get insolvency analysis, but the operator holding locked collateral — who becomes administrator — is thinner. (2) Cross-jurisdiction finality and tax: when finality attaches, and how bridge events are taxed, vary by jurisdiction and stay unresolved. (3) Stablecoin perimeter vs licensing reality: practice runs ahead of the whitelist, a regulatory catch-up risk the paper does not price.
