01What this is

The first tokenised-SGD proof of concept (MAS-commissioned, 2017; authored with and by Deloitte and R3). Banks pledge cash to MAS and receive a central-bank-backed digital SGD 1:1, then transfer it peer-to-peer on Ethereum-lineage DLT without an intermediary. The tech was a solved problem even in 2017; the vendor framing (“Internet of Value”, a cited WEF claim that “80% of banks will initiate DLT projects in 2017”) is consultant marketing layered over a genuine experiment.

02Why it matters

Its value is genealogical, not technical. Ubin P1 founds the MAS-convened repertory cast — eleven FIs plus Deloitte/R3 — whose names recur for a decade: Naveen Mallela (JPM, later head of Onyx); BCS the clearing incumbent; SGX; the local banks. Read the report as the cap-table of Singapore’s digital-money establishment. The pledge model (collateral locked, representation circulates) is also the synthetic-CBDC pattern that PBM later generalises.

03Key findings

Finding 1 — The pledge model is a synthetic CBDC. Cash pledged to MAS, tokens issued 1:1 against it — functionally a fully-reserved stablecoin run inside the central bank. The collateral-locked / representation-circulates pattern PBM inherits is born here.
Finding 2 — The consortium is the product. Eleven institutions learning together under MAS; the durable deliverable was relationships and shared vocabulary, not software. Ubin P1’s real output was the repertory company that authors everything after it.
Finding 3 — Vendor-authored state research. Deloitte’s brand and worldview saturate a central-bank publication; the hype baseline dates instantly. Reliability discipline: separate the experiment (real) from the narrative (sold) — and note the record of what “succeeded” is written by the party paid to deliver it.

05The Money Trail

Nobody makes money from this 2017 pilot itself; the money is in what the roster becomes. The report reads as a neutral technical exercise, but it is really the founding cap-table of a decade-long programme, and the individuals are the tell. Toh Wee Kee authors Ubin as MAS and later signs Guardian’s framework as JPMorgan; Naveen Mallela sits in the JPM seat here and later runs Onyx — the same Onyx that co-authors the public PBM standard (P1) its network then sells into. The consortium narrows over the phases to MAS-plus-Temasek-plus-JPMorgan, and the Partior JV (JPM/DBS/Temasek) commercialises the pledge-model rails within a year (P10). None of this is alleged impropriety; it is structure — public research to private company to public research’s vendor, with the revolving door disclosed nowhere in the paper itself.

06The Gap

What a founding document leaves unwritten. (1) No exit analysis: nothing on what unwinding tokenised SGD does to reserve accounting. (2) Legal status of the pledged-token claim: never characterised, then or since. (3) Vendor-capture of the record: each Ubin phase is authored by the consultant paid to deliver it (Deloitte → Accenture → Deloitte), so the official history of what “worked” is written by the parties positioned to monetise success — a conflict the reports never disclose.
