Stage 1 — Intake Analysis

SUBJECT: "Central bank digital currencies" — CPMI & Markets Committee Papers No 174, 12 Mar 2018, 34 pages. The first official BIS framework on CBDCs — the document that begins the official arc on the timeline. SOURCE / RELIABILITY: Primary — BIS first-party publication. Highest tier. ENTITIES: CPMI (chair: Benoît Cœuré — reappears as co-chair of the 2020 principles report [X4] and head of the BIS Innovation Hub) · Markets Committee (chair: Jacqueline Loh — Deputy Managing Director of MAS. A Singapore central banker co-fronts the first global CBDC framework while MAS is two years into Project Ubin [P5]). KEY CLAIMS: establishes the wholesale-vs-general-purpose CBDC taxonomy · a general-purpose CBDC could make commercial banks' "reliance on customer deposits... less stable, as deposits could more easily take flight to the central bank in times of stress" · monetary-policy transmission could be affected · every jurisdiction should "carefully and thoroughly consider the implications before making any decision." Companion op-ed by Cœuré & Loh: "Bitcoin not the answer to a cashless society." SIGNIFICANCE: the taxonomy and the caution here govern everything downstream — the wholesale track becomes Ubin, the retail track becomes Orchid, and the deposit-protection concern becomes doctrine. ROUTE: → timeline anchor, Mar 2018. File as the opening move of the official response.

Stage 2 — Knowledge Map

Executive Summary (Alphas)

Alpha 1 — The split that organised a decade. Wholesale vs general-purpose is drawn here, and the whole MAS programme obeys it: Ubin runs the wholesale track [P5]–[P10], Orchid the retail one [P2]–[P4]. A 2018 committee taxonomy became the org chart of Singapore's build. Alpha 2 — Deposit protection is born as doctrine. The paper's core worry — deposits fleeing to the central bank — is the same protective logic MAS formalises three years later in its retail-CBDC economics monograph [P3] (deposits are 92% of money; protect the funding base first). The constitution of the eventual system is written here, as a risk paragraph. Alpha 3 — The Singapore membrane, first sighting. Loh's chair seat means Singapore was not following the BIS arc — it was co-writing it, two years into its own wholesale pilots. On the brief's timeline, the "BIS papers" lane and the "Singapore" lane are not parallel tracks; they share personnel.

Stage 3 — Research Note

What the paper does — and what it will not touch

D174 does three things: defines the object, warns about the banks, and licenses investigation ("more work is needed"). What it studiously avoids is the citizen: there is no treatment of consent, of programmability of retail money, of what conditions attached to public money would mean for the people holding it. The omission is structural and it never gets repaired — five years later the PBM whitepaper ships expiry, whitelists and revocation as creator-set parameters P1, and the citizen-sentiment study still does not exist (the confirmed negative across all 21 MAS papers).

The ten-year read: d174's "no decision to issue" caution became the cover under which capability-building proceeded everywhere. Say nothing is decided; build everything required. MAS later gave this posture a name — optionality [P2][P3]. The first BIS CBDC paper is where the posture is licensed.
