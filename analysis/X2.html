01What this is

CPMI & Markets Committee Papers No 174, 12 Mar 2018, 34 pages — the first official BIS framework on CBDCs. It establishes the wholesale-vs-general-purpose taxonomy, warns that a general-purpose CBDC could make bank deposits “take flight to the central bank in times of stress,” and licenses investigation while formally deciding nothing.

02Why it matters

The taxonomy and caution here govern everything downstream — the wholesale track becomes Ubin P5, the retail track becomes Orchid P4, and the deposit-protection worry becomes MAS doctrine P3. The people matter as much as the paper: CPMI chair Benoît Cœuré reappears co-chairing the 2020 principles X4 and running the BIS Innovation Hub; Markets Committee chair Jacqueline Loh was Deputy Managing Director of MAS.

03Key findings

Finding 1 — The split that organised a decade. Wholesale vs general-purpose is drawn here, and the whole MAS programme obeys it: Ubin runs the wholesale track P5, Orchid the retail one P4. A 2018 committee taxonomy became the org chart of Singapore's build.
Finding 2 — Deposit protection born as doctrine. The core worry — deposits fleeing to the central bank — is the same protective logic MAS formalises three years later P3: protect the bank funding base first. The constitution of the eventual system is written here as a risk paragraph.
Finding 3 — The Singapore membrane, first sighting. Loh's chair seat means Singapore was not following the BIS arc — it was co-writing it, two years into its own wholesale pilots. The “BIS papers” lane and the “Singapore” lane share personnel.

05The Money Trail

No commercial money changes hands, so the trail is one of influence and personnel. The individuals who chair the first framework recirculate into the bodies that write the next one — Cœuré → X4 and the BIS Innovation Hub — and into the jurisdiction that deploys first — Loh at MAS, mid-Ubin P5. The non-disclosure is not a hidden payment but the un-flagged continuity: the same small cast acquiring larger mandates while the papers read as neutral committee output. On the timeline, the “BIS lane” and the “Singapore lane” are not parallel — they share people.

06The Gap

Three silences. (1) The citizen: no treatment of consent, of retail programmability, or of what conditions attached to public money would mean for holders — an omission never repaired P1. (2) “No decision to issue” became the cover under which capability-building proceeded everywhere — say nothing is decided, build everything required; MAS later names this posture optionality P3. (3) Continuity unnamed: the Cœuré / Loh recirculation is nowhere framed as continuity.
