01What this is

The Guardian FX workstream applies shared ledgers and tokenised bank liabilities (TBLs) to cross-border corporate transaction banking and FX. It re-maps the payment lifecycle for 24/7 settlement, carries live-shaped use cases from Ant International, BNY+OCBC and HSBC, and pushes an agenda to standardise tokenised-FX documentation atop existing industry standards. TBLs are defined as bank-balance-sheet claims, deliberately distinct from stablecoins and CBDCs.

02Why it matters

This is where wholesale research becomes bank product. Among the Orchid Blueprint's three permitted monies P4, TBLs are the one shipping in commercial use cases — CBDC deferred, stablecoins licensed slowly — vindicating the 2021 deposit-protection doctrine P3 in a market outcome. The documentation push is ISDA-style infrastructure: whoever writes the master terms writes the market.

03Key findings

Finding 1 — TBLs win the money race by default. Of the Blueprint's permitted monies P4, tokenised bank liabilities are the ones live in corporate use cases. The two-tier system's digital heir is the bank deposit, exactly as the retail-CBDC doctrine P3 predicted.
Finding 2 — Ant inside the tent. A platform giant's treasury operating on bank-issued tokenised money under MAS convening is co-option in miniature: big tech joins the rails as client, not issuer — the walled-garden threat neutralised by membership.
Finding 3 — Documentation is destiny. The standardised tokenised-FX documentation agenda is market infrastructure. The drafting venue and drafters are not named in the paper, yet those master terms will bind every corporate that signs — the ISDA-analogue to watch.

05The Money Trail

The workstream presents TBLs and three use cases as neutral plumbing. As a money map, the banks that co-shape the standard (OCBC, HSBC, BNY) are the same institutions that issue and settle the very TBLs the standard blesses; P12 — the consortium rail whose owners overlap the Guardian banks P10 — is the unnamed production venue the flow lands on. Ant enters as client, but at platform scale could internalise FX flow rivalling mid-tier banks. The non-disclosure sits in the gap between “co-author of the tokenised-FX standard” and “issuer and settler of the money it standardises” — positioning, not wrongdoing, but undisclosed in-paper. The trail points onward to the consortium map in P10 and P12.

06The Gap

Three things the paper never resolves. (1) Whose master documentation: the drafting venue and drafters are unnamed, yet these terms will govern every corporate contract. (2) The co-option number: Ant-scale internalisable FX volume — the figure that would show whether client-not-issuer holds — is absent. (3) 24/7 risk redesign: continuous settlement abolishes the overnight batch every current risk and ops model assumes; the paper lists risks but not the institutional redesign continuous FX implies.
