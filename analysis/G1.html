01What this is

An industry framework for tokenised fixed income, convened under Project Guardian. It maps the full bond lifecycle (primary, secondary, maturity), sets out operating archetypes and a data model aligned to the ICMA Bond Data Taxonomy, and adds addenda on DvP settlement and custody, with case studies including SGX digital bonds, HSBC pilots and Project Trident. Unusually candid, it diagnoses the adoption gap directly: weak business case against efficient incumbents, legal fragmentation across jurisdictions, infrastructure immaturity, and — the keystone — the absence of cash on chain that blocks lifecycle automation.

02Why it matters

This is where the corpus's two halves declare their coupling. Tokenised bonds cannot self-execute coupons and redemptions without tokenised money on the same ledger — so Guardian's asset track openly depends on the Orchid money track (P4). The framework also punctures the disintermediation myth in writing: clients still want licensed custodians "contrary to what is sometimes claimed." Read forensically, the paper standardises a market that mostly re-centres the incumbents tokenisation rhetoric promised to remove — and hands whoever owns the taxonomy the integration layer of a trillion-dollar asset class.

03Key findings

Finding 1 — "Absence of cash on chain" is the keystone admission. Bonds can't automate their own coupons and redemptions without tokenised money beside them. Guardian's supply-side build therefore needs Orchid's demand-side money — the frameworks are one machine, and G1 is where that dependency is admitted in writing.
Finding 2 — Custody realism deflates the disintermediation myth. The framework states clients want licensed custodians, full stop. Tokenisation re-centres exactly the intermediaries it promised to remove; incumbency absorbs the innovation and keeps the fees.
Finding 3 — Standards are the moat-leveller — and the land-grab. ICMA Bond Data Taxonomy adoption stops issuers minting incompatible bond tokens. Taxonomy first, market second: whoever's data standard becomes default owns the layer every issuer must integrate against.

05The Money Trail

Follow the incumbency. The framework asks issuers and banks to fund a migration whose float and fee income the migration itself erodes — yet it never prices that cannibalisation, which is the real adoption gap. Meanwhile the parties named as case studies — SGX (listing and registry economics) and HSBC (custody, issuance, settlement) — are positioned to monetise the standard they help demonstrate, with no in-paper disclosure of that downstream interest. The custody addendum quietly restores licensed custodians to the centre, so the intermediary fees survive the "disintermediation." And the ICMA taxonomy is a soft chokepoint: whoever's data model wins collects the integration tax. The cash-leg dependency points straight at the Orchid money forms (P4), and the barrier list generalises into the buy-side adoption thesis of G4, while the rails these bonds settle on lead to P12.

06The Gap

What the framework never publishes. (1) Unit economics: cost-benefit is asserted qualitatively — the tokenised-vs-conventional issuance number that would settle the business-case debate is absent across the industry. (2) Cannibalisation: the framework never prices the float and fees migration destroys for the very parties asked to fund it. (3) Coupon-automation liability: who answers when a smart-contract coupon misfires is left unstated, custody addendum notwithstanding.
